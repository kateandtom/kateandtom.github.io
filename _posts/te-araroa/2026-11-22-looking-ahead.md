We’re planning to walk north from Bluff to Cape Reinga, starting on 1 January 2027. What might the journey feel like along the way? These three views give a rough sense of the people, weather and ground under our feet.

The plan assumes **17 miles per calendar day**, including rest days, over approximately 1,864 trail miles. It is a straight-line planning model, not our live location or an itinerary: weather, rest, transfers and route changes will alter where we actually are. ## How to read this

The hiker bands and busyness curve are illustrative, not counts or probabilities. They combine estimated northbound and southbound through-walker positions with a fixed, invented baseline for day and section walkers. Popular places such as Tongariro have a higher local-use peak; weekends, holidays, weather and hut capacity are not modelled. The [Te Araroa Trust](https://www.teararoa.org.nz/ways-to-walk/full-trail/) describes typical starting seasons, and its [walker survey](https://www.teararoa.org.nz/ta-walker-survey/) reports an average of 132 for full-trail walkers; we interpret this as days for the model. The survey does not specify the unit in that sentence. The model’s peaks and ranges are judgement calls, not measured crowds.

Weather figures are **1991–2020 station averages** from [Earth Sciences New Zealand / NIWA](https://niwa.co.nz/climate-and-weather/climate-data-and-activities). Each broad stretch uses one representative town station, sometimes a long way from the route. Bars show average total rainfall for a whole month, not rain per walking day; temperatures are mean daily lows and highs. These are not forecasts, and exposed mountains and valleys can be much colder, wetter or windier than the station. Some station averages use fewer than 30 complete years of data.

Terrain descriptions and boundaries are broad approximations based on the [Te Araroa sections](https://www.teararoa.org.nz/ways-to-walk/full-trail/) and [trail notes and maps](https://www.teararoa.org.nz/notes-and-maps/), not a turn-by-turn guide. Check current trail alerts, river levels and forecasts before setting out. The Cook Strait boundary compresses the transfer between Ship Cove, Picton and Wellington; trail miles are approximate planning coordinates.
